About this map

What it shows

The map shows growing conditions in the forests of Cyprus, today. It colours each forest square of 250 m from poor to very good.

It never shows where mushrooms are. It never tells you what a mushroom is. It never says a mushroom can be eaten.

Double tap to zoom in. Close up, the forest shows shade in 50 m squares: cool slopes, valley bottoms and little autumn sun. Tap a square to see its growing conditions.

Nature reserves are closed to picking. They are grey with lines and always score 0.

What goes into the score

Three things matter most.

  • Trees. Our mushrooms grow with partner trees: pine and golden oak. No partner tree, no score.
  • Recent rain. The ground needs a good soaking. A big rain 1 to 3 weeks ago helps most.
  • Temperature. Mild days are best. Too hot or too cold, and the score drops.

Wet soil, the right month for the height, and shade add a little more. Tap the rating word on a square to see every part.

The mushroom clock counts the days since the last soaking rain. It tells you if the fruiting window is open.

Where the data comes from

  • Weather: Open-Meteo, a free weather service.
  • Trees: the Copernicus forest map of Europe, from 2018.
  • Heights and slopes: the Copernicus height map of Europe.
  • Golden oak zone: the rock map of the Geological Survey Department of Cyprus.
  • Burned forest: EFFIS satellite fire maps, since 2018.
  • Nature reserves: the European list of protected areas (EEA).
  • Roads, tracks and the base map: OpenStreetMap.
  • Mushroom photos: iNaturalist. Each photo shows its author and licence.
  • Public records on the mushroom cards: GBIF and iNaturalist (counts and links only).

The honest limits

  • The weather is coarse. Each weather square is about 9 km wide. Rain in the mountains can be very different a few kilometres away.
  • The trees are from a 2018 map. Forests change. Some trees may be gone, or new.
  • A good square can still be empty. Good conditions do not mean mushrooms are there.

Use the map to choose where and when to look. Never to decide what to pick.

The law in the forest

Picking is allowed in state forests without a permit, but not in nature reserves.

Rakes and tools are banned: up to 1 year in prison, up to €5,000 fine, or both.

No fires in forests or within 2 km of them.

Pick by hand or knife. Put the leaf litter back.

Poisoning

Call 112 (ambulance) or the Cyprus Poison Centre 1401. Do not wait for symptoms. Bring the mushroom to the hospital.
