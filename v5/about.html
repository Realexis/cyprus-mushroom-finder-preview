About the data

This map shows growing conditions in Cyprus forests: where rain, temperature, soil moisture, season and shade are right for wild mushrooms to fruit. It does not show where mushrooms are, and it says nothing about any mushroom you find.

How the score works

Each 250 m forest square gets a score from 0 to 100, every day. Six things count:

  • Rain, last 30 days (20%): 0 below 20 mm, full at 80 mm.
  • A soaking rain 7–20 days ago (15%): biggest 3-day total. 0 below 10 mm, full at 25 mm.
  • Temperature, last 7 days (25%): best 8–18 °C, zero at 3 °C and 24 °C. Frost lowers it.
  • Soil moisture (15%): today compared with the same place in the autumns and winters of 2024-25 and 2025-26.
  • Season for the altitude (15%): high Troodos starts earlier, low hills later. The season runs September to April.
  • Shade (10%): north/east slopes, valley bottoms and little autumn sun.

No soaking rain, low score. The score is then multiplied by the biggest 3-day rain of the last 30 days, divided by 20 mm (never more than 1). One soaking rain of 20 mm or more: no cut. No rain: almost no score. Rain older than 20 days counts a little less each day, so scores fade instead of dropping overnight. The mushroom clock uses the same rain, so the two always agree.

Mushroom clock. The season's first fruiting window opens about 18 days after the first soaking rain (20 mm or more) since 1 September. After that, each new soaking rain opens it again after about 2 days. It closes about 20 days after the latest soaking rain. The clock is a guide in words; it does not change the score. Until our rain record reaches back to 1 September, the clock counts from the first day it has, and says so.

Burned forest. Forest more than half burned since 2018 (EFFIS satellite fire maps) no longer counts for the tree-partner species. For morels it counts in the first two springs after the fire.

Partner trees only. The general map counts pine and golden oak forest. "Other broadleaf" forest is not scored there (no partner tree for our autumn species), but it counts for morels.

Species filters change only two things: which trees and altitudes count, and the season months. They say "good conditions for [species] habitat". They never say a species is there.

Squares with no forest score 0. Nature reserves are closed and always score 0. Temperature is corrected for height (−6.5 °C per 1,000 m). Future days use the weather forecast, so confidence drops after 2 days.

The map shows every 250 m forest square. The weather behind each square comes from a model about 9 km wide, so the fine detail comes from the forest itself: trees, altitude and shade. In the top zones you can also zoom into 50 m terrain (shade only, no finer weather).

The map shows growing conditions only. It never shows where mushrooms are, and never anyone's finds.

How sure is this map?

It is a best estimate, not a promise.

  • The rain numbers come from a weather model with squares about 9 km wide. Rain in the mountains can change faster than that.
  • The timing rules come from field practice in Cyprus and studies in Spain.
  • Very few mushroom finds are recorded in Cyprus, so we tested the map on last season instead. In the dry autumn of 2025, the old map said "Fair" or better on over half the forest. This one stayed low. In the wet winter that followed, the two maps are the same.
  • We also looked at the 18 dated public finds of that season. A test this small can catch a clearly wrong map. It cannot prove a good one.

Use it to choose where and when to look, never what to pick.

The law and the forest

Picking is allowed in state forests without a permit, except in nature reserves.

Rakes and tools are banned: up to 1 year in prison, up to €5,000 fine, or both.

No fires in forests or within 2 km of them.

Pick by hand or knife. Put the leaf litter back.
